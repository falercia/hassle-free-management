## Suggested LinkedIn caption

A tactical meeting is not accountability theater. It is a course correction.

It works in the short and medium term and translates what strategy decided into plan, people and resources. Managers and leaders who turn direction into a practical program take part, usually once a week. The agenda covers results, deviations, plan adjustments, reallocation of people and, when needed, a change of course. It has more debate than the operational meeting, and that is why it needs more preparation.

The most common mistake is turning the weekly into a slide parade. Each area presents its own report, time runs out before the debate, and next week's plan comes out the same as the last one, only with a bigger drift. Patrick Lencioni, in Death by Meeting, proposes the opposite, a quick round of one minute per person, an agenda built on the spot from whatever drifted, and every strategic topic parked for the right meeting.
