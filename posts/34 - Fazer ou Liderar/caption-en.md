**Pillar:** 06 · The Leader's Time and Focus
**In this post:** when the leader should get hands on, and what the four legitimate reasons are.

## Suggested LinkedIn caption

Getting hands on is not wrong. What is wrong is doing it out of exhaustion, anxiety or distrust, and calling it setting an example.

There are four legitimate reasons for a leader to execute. A real emergency, when there is no time to hand it over. Teaching by doing, when someone needs to see how it is done. Setting the culture, when the gesture says more than any speech. And the call only you can make, the one carrying risk only you can hold. Outside that list, a manager executing is usually an escape from the work of leading, which is more uncomfortable and less rewarding in the short run.

The warning sign is repetition. If you did the same thing for the team three times in one month, that was not an emergency, it was a missing process or missing people. And while you keep patching the hole with your own hands, nobody in the company will see that the hole exists.
