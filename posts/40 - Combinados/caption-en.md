## Suggested LinkedIn caption

The value of an agreement is not set on the day it is made. It is set the first time someone breaks it.

Everyone invests energy in the opening conversation, the nice alignment, the well written minutes. Then the first break arrives, someone does not deliver, and the manager runs the silent math, today is not the day, this one was not worth it, I will let this one go. In that instant the whole team learns what an agreement is worth in that area, and it is not what was written down.

Letting it go is not generosity, it is a management decision with consequences. Whoever did deliver sees that delivering was optional, whoever did not sees that there was no cost, and the next agreement is born worth less than the last one.

Before you demand anything, four conditions need to exist: the agreement was built together, it is written and visible, it has an owner with a name and not a department, and it has a review date. Without that there is nothing to protect.
