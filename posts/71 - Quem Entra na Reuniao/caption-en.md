# It Has a Name · Management Made Simple · #71 · Who Gets Into the Meeting

**Pillar:** 03 · Execution and Process
**In this post:** the Surgical Invite, the four seats that justify someone being in a meeting, and the open door test, Elon Musk's rule for whoever is in the room for no reason.

## Suggested LinkedIn caption

A meeting invite is not a courtesy. It is a cost.

Everyone has left a meeting tired, with nothing new to do and the feeling of having lost an hour of their life. That is not bad luck. It is someone who did not have the courage to leave you out. The guest list grows out of fear, so as not to exclude whoever looks important or whoever "might be useful", and every extra name dilutes the agenda, opens a side conversation and pushes the decision to the next meeting.

The criterion that fixes this fits in four seats. Who decides, who executes, who holds the data nobody can decide without, and who pays the bill for the decision in their own area. Whoever fits none of them gets the summary the same day, with the decision, an owner and a deadline. Out of the hour, never out of the information.

And for whoever is already in the room for no reason, there is the rule Elon Musk sent in writing to his entire company: once it is clear you are not adding value, get up and leave. Leaving is not rude. Making someone stay is.
