## Suggested LinkedIn caption

A decision does not need to be understood by the people in the room. It needs to survive the third person who repeats it.

That is where almost every manager's communication breaks. You explain it well, the people listening understand, and three days later the version circulating on the front line has nothing to do with what was decided. It is not bad faith, it is the route, each person passes on what they found most important and cuts the rest, and the rest was precisely the context.

A decision that survives repetition fits in four lines, what changes, starting when, who does what, and what does not change. The fourth is the one nobody writes and the one that prevents panic, because in its absence the team assumes everything is up for grabs.
