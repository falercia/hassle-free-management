**In this post:** how to leave firefighting mode and start planning again, using the two clocks.

## Suggested LinkedIn caption

You are not going to stop fighting fires, and that is not the goal either. The goal is to stop living only in that.

There are two clocks running in your week. The first is the urgency clock, reactive, loud, and it will always exist. The second is the root clock, quiet, with no deadline pushing it, which is why it is the first thing sacrificed. Whoever never starts the second clock solves the same problems forever, with teams that get more tired every quarter.

The detail almost every manager ignores is that the root cannot be investigated during the crisis. Those are two different modes, and analysis done in the middle of the fire produces a rushed conclusion, almost always blaming people instead of design. That is why the second clock needs a block on the calendar, with data on the table and far from the noise.
